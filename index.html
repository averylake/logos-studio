The Logos

The Logos is a series of typographic portraits of Christ assembled from the scripts through which he was written, transmitted, and imagined: Greek, Hebrew, Cyrillic, and Ethiopic. Rendered after Rembrandt’s Head of Christ series (c. 1648), the works bring ancient text into an artificial architecture.

Every generation represents Christ through the medium available to it. The Gospel writers shaped him in narrative. Rembrandt in light and shadow. This series is built through code for the age of language models.

Every representation carries three layers of bias: the author who frames it, the medium that translates it, and the viewer who completes it. Here, those layers become the selected source, the code that interprets it, and the collector’s choices.

The machine does not reveal a neutral image of the Logos. It returns a reflection shaped by what we place before it. Architect Editions exposes those assumptions and records them as part of the work.

Avery Lake, 2026
